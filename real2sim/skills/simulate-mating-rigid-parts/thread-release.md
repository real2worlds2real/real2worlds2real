# Diagnose thread engagement and release

Use this guidance when a contact-driven threaded pair stalls or fails to
separate despite apparently matching mating profiles.

Inspect the actual collision surfaces through the full exit path. Matching
handedness, lead and engaged clearance do not guarantee release: an unthreaded
outlet or abrupt groove termination can block the moving crest. Compare the
crest's swept envelope with the exit bore before increasing torque or reducing
friction. Inspect collider approximation and contact margins as well as the
visible mesh; a rendered section alone does not establish collision clearance.

Test a proposed geometric repair with the previous drive and numerical settings
first when feasible. Keep the failed motion for comparison. Leave axial motion
free during engagement, and make external fixtures or gravity compensation
explicit; prescribing height from rotation cannot validate thread-driven travel.

Measure axial travel against unwrapped relative rotation over an interval with
actual engaged motion. A stationary part can have a small lead residual. Review
release separately, including finite thread ends and sustained contact loss.
Optional rotation-resisted pull or ridge-removed controls can distinguish the
mechanism from the fixture. Retain actual angular drift and mass properties;
changing collision geometry can change inertia as well as contact.

A cutaway used for visual inspection must not silently remove collision
surfaces. Replay actual simulated motion and repeat source-matched checks after
geometry changes. Successful contact in one backend does not validate another.
